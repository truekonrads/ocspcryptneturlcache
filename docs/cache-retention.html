Expired is not deleted

Windows normally retains an OCSP response beyond the time when it can satisfy a revocation check. The difference creates a useful—but conditional—forensic window.

A normal entry becomes eligible for deletion after the later of its response expiry and last synchronization time, plus a flush exemption that defaults to 28 days. A later Cryptnet service scan performs the deletion.

Two different clocks. Protocol expiry answers whether Windows can reuse the response. Flush eligibility answers when the files may be removed. Neither timestamp proves when a website was visited or malware executed.

Interpret presence cautiously

At day 30, an entry with a seven-day response interval is expected to remain under the ordinary time calculation. It does not identify the process or prove a connection to a candidate hostname.

Eligibility is not deletion. Manual clearing, profile cleanup, no-cache flags, or collection from the wrong identity can shorten or eliminate the observable window.

The calculation

For the normal service branch, the later of expiry and last synchronization becomes the base for the exemption:

valid_until  = min(nextUpdate, certificate.NotAfter)
              or min(thisUpdate + 12h, certificate.NotAfter)

flush_base  = max(ExpireTime, LastSyncTime)
eligible_at = flush_base + exemption       # normally 28 days

Active prefetch can refresh an entry and move LastSyncTime without the original process running. An exemption of 0xFFFFFFFF disables automatic time-based flushing.
